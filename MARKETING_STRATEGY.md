## 1. Objective

The marketing objective is deliberately narrow:

> **Acquire 30 paying TariffCompass Business customers and 3 paying TariffCompass Advisor customers as quickly as possible.**

This is the commercial milestone that precedes crowdfunding preparation.

At standard pricing:

- 30 Business × C$99/month = **C$2,970 MRR**
- 3 Advisor × C$249/month = **C$747 MRR**
- Total normalized MRR = **C$3,717**
- Total normalized ARR = **C$44,604**

Actual billed MRR will be lower while founding discounts are active. Actual billed recurring revenue and normalized full-price MRR must always be tracked separately.

The first 10 Business customers receive the founding offer of 50% off for 12 months. The first 3 Advisor customers receive the founding offer of 50% off for 12 months.

The goal is not maximum traffic, followers, registrations, press mentions, or free accounts. The goal is **33 paying accounts with evidence of recurring value**.

## 2. Customer priority

### Priority 1 — Business customers

Target Canadian SMEs with material cross-border trade exposure, especially where tariffs affect meaningful dollars rather than trivial transaction values.

Highest-priority profiles:

1. Canadian manufacturers exporting to the United States.
2. Canadian manufacturers importing material inputs.
3. Wholesalers and distributors importing products for resale.
4. Import-dependent retailers with meaningful sourcing exposure.
5. Businesses affected by Section 232, counter-tariffs, surtaxes, or other current high-profile measures.
6. Businesses with several products/HS codes or multiple trade routes where monitoring has recurring value.

Avoid spending significant founder time on businesses with negligible cross-border volume unless they are unusually influential referral sources.

### Priority 2 — Advisor customers

Target accountants and fractional CFO/advisory firms with commercial SME client books.

The Advisor proposition is not generic tariff research. It is:

> **Know which clients were affected by a trade-policy change, how material the exposure may be, and who needs a conversation first.**

Advisor acquisition should not delay Business launch. Begin serious Advisor conversion once the single-business product is credible and a useful multi-client experience exists.

---

## 3. Core sales proposition

TariffCompass should be sold on financial relevance, not information access.

### Business proposition

Lead with:

> **Give us one product you import or export and your approximate annual trade value. TariffCompass will show what tariff/trade-policy treatment applies, what changed, the official source, and the estimated financial impact.**

Then sell recurring monitoring:

> **TariffCompass will keep watching that exposure and alert you when something material changes.**

The product must feel like C$99 of decision intelligence, not C$99 of information.

### Advisor proposition

Lead with:

> **TariffCompass helps you identify which clients are exposed to tariff changes and approximately how much is at stake, so you know who needs attention first.**

The Advisor product should be sold as portfolio relevance and triage, not merely white-label reports.

## 4. Acquisition sequence

Use a staged approach. Do not scale paid acquisition before founder-led conversion is understood.

### Stage A — Pre-launch pipeline while product gates are closing

Objective: build the first prospect pool before launch.

Actions:

- Build a manually curated list of **100–150 Business prospects**.
- Build a list of **20–30 accounting/fractional-CFO firms**.
- Prioritize companies visibly exposed to current tariff headlines.
- Capture contact name, title, company, sector, likely trade direction, likely affected products, email/LinkedIn, source, status, and next action.
- Identify warm introductions through existing business, banking, accounting, commercial real-estate, professional, board, and local business networks where appropriate.
- Begin low-pressure discovery conversations before launch without selling a product that is not ready.

Deliverable before launch: at least **30 warm/qualified Business prospects** that can be contacted immediately when Beta opens.

### Stage B — Founding 10 Business customers

Objective: fill the first 10 founding Business seats quickly and learn aggressively.

Primary channels:

1. Founder network and direct introductions.
2. Highly personalized outbound email/LinkedIn.
3. Direct calls to businesses with obvious tariff exposure.
4. Local and regional chambers/business associations where access is easy.
5. Referrals from accountants, bankers, brokers, trade professionals, and existing customers.

Do not lead with “50% off.” Lead with the business problem and demo. Use the founding offer only after value is demonstrated.

Every founding customer should receive a founder interview.

Key questions after purchase:

- What triggered you to care about tariffs now?
- Which TariffCompass output made the product worth paying for?
- What almost stopped you from subscribing?
- Does C$99/month feel trivial, fair, or expensive relative to the exposure shown?
- What would make you cancel?
- Which products/HS codes should TariffCompass monitor?
- Would you refer another trade-exposed business?

Goal: **10 paying Business customers + 5 strong quotes/case-study candidates.**

### Stage C — Business customers 11–30

Objective: prove that full-price C$99 converts without founding pricing.

This stage is the real pricing validation.

Channels, in order:

1. Referral loop from founding customers.
2. Founder-led outbound to lookalike businesses.
3. Accountants/bookkeepers/fractional CFOs as referral partners.
4. Trade associations/chambers/manufacturing groups where access is efficient.
5. High-intent Google Search.
6. LinkedIn retargeting and narrowly targeted campaigns if economics justify them.
7. Public insights/updates that produce inbound demand.

Do not materially scale a channel until there is evidence it produces qualified demos or paid customers.

Goal: **20 additional Business customers at standard C$99 pricing.**

### Stage D — First 3 Advisor customers

Objective: obtain 3 paying Advisor accounts at C$249 standard pricing, subject to the first-3 founding offer for the first 12 months.

Ideal profile:

- accounting/fractional-CFO firm;
- meaningful commercial SME book;
- several clients that import/export;
- partner or senior advisor willing to test on real clients;
- sees value in proactive client conversations.

Sales motion:

1. Identify 5–10 potentially exposed clients together.
2. Run TariffCompass across a representative subset.
3. Show portfolio-level affected/not-affected view.
4. Demonstrate how the advisor can identify who needs attention first.
5. Convert only when recurring portfolio monitoring is credible.

Goal: **3 paying Advisor accounts + at least 1 Advisor testimonial.**

## 5. Funnel math

The working funnel must be measured and recalibrated weekly.

### Initial planning assumptions — Business

These are hypotheses, not promises:

- 100 targeted prospects contacted
- 25–35 meaningful replies/conversations
- 15–20 demos
- 5–8 paid customers

That implies a rough targeted-outbound demo-to-paid conversion objective of **25–40%** once a prospect is genuinely qualified and sees a material exposure.

To acquire 30 Business customers, plan initially for approximately:

- **400–600 well-targeted prospects**
- **100–150 meaningful conversations**
- **60–90 demos**

If actual conversion is materially weaker after the first 15–20 demos, fix positioning/product before increasing volume.

### Initial planning assumptions — Advisor

Because this is founder-led and relationship-driven, target:

- 15–20 carefully selected firms
- 8–10 serious conversations
- 5–6 demos/design-partner sessions
- 3 paid Advisor accounts

Do not mass-market Advisor before the workflow is demonstrable.

## 6. Founder-led outbound playbook

### Prospect research

Before contacting a business, identify at least one plausible reason tariffs matter to them:

- U.S. export exposure;
- imported inputs;
- affected sector;
- current tariff headline;
- product category likely subject to a measure;
- sourcing concentration; or
- public evidence of cross-border activity.

Avoid generic “we built a tariff tool” outbound.

### First-touch structure

Keep outreach short:

1. Why this specific company may be affected.
2. What TariffCompass can show in one short demo.
3. Ask for 15–20 minutes.

Do not send long product descriptions before the meeting.

### Demo structure

Target **10–15 minutes**.

1. Enter one real product/HS code.
2. Show current treatment.
3. Show source/effective date/confidence.
4. Show estimated annual financial impact.
5. Show what changed if applicable.
6. Show practical response options.
7. Show monitoring/alert value.
8. Ask whether this is worth C$99/month to keep watching.

The demo should demonstrate value before discussing discount.

### Follow-up cadence

Default founder cadence:

- Day 0: personalized outreach.
- Day 3: concise follow-up with one relevant tariff fact or insight.
- Day 7: final short follow-up / offer to run one product for them.
- After demo: same-day summary + payment/signup link.
- Day 2 after demo: answer objections.
- Day 7 after demo: close or explicitly park.

Do not run endless follow-up sequences on low-intent prospects.

## 7. Referral engine

Every paying customer should be treated as a potential acquisition channel.

After the customer receives clear value, ask:

> **Do you know one other Canadian business that imports or exports and is dealing with the same uncertainty?**

Do not initially build a complex affiliate program.

Track:

- customer source;
- referrer;
- referred demos;
- referred paid customers.

If referrals become a meaningful share of acquisition, formalize the program after the first 30 customers.

Advisor firms can become especially powerful referral/distribution partners because one relationship can expose TariffCompass to multiple SMEs.

## 8. Paid advertising strategy

Early policy:

> **Recycle available operating cash into acquisition, but scale ads only after a channel demonstrates measurable conversion.**

### Channel 1 — Google Search

Highest initial paid priority because intent can be explicit.

Test terms around:

- Canada tariff calculator
- US tariffs Canadian goods
- Canadian importer tariff
- Canadian exporter tariff
- tariff exposure calculator
- HS code tariff Canada
- Section 232 Canada
- Canada counter tariffs
- tariff impact business

Landing pages should match intent and lead quickly to a free analysis/demo.

### Channel 2 — Retargeting

Retarget visitors who:

- complete or partially complete analysis;
- visit pricing;
- view tariff updates or insights;
- return multiple times.

Keep frequency controlled. The objective is conversion, not impression volume.

### Channel 3 — LinkedIn

Use narrowly, not as broad awareness spend.

Potential targeting:

- owner/founder;
- CFO/controller;
- operations/supply-chain leaders;
- manufacturers, wholesalers, distributors;
- accounting firm partners and fractional CFOs.

Use LinkedIn more for account targeting and retargeting than generic display.

### Paid acquisition guardrails

At C$99/month, track:

- CAC by channel;
- trial/demo-to-paid conversion;
- first-90-day churn;
- CAC payback;
- gross-margin payback;
- customer quality by channel.

Do not scale simply because clicks are cheap.

Initial goal: keep CAC low enough that payback is plausibly within **3–6 months**, while accepting some temporary inefficiency during learning.

Pause or redesign any campaign that generates traffic but not qualified analysis/demo activity.

## 9. Public authority as acquisition support

The public research layer supports the 30+3 objective but must not become a separate marketing project.

Use `/insights`, `/updates`, and `/sources` for:

- current tariff developments;
- sourced before/after changes;
- representative SME financial-impact examples;
- selected sector exposure;
- timelines and effective dates;
- methodology/source transparency.

Each piece should include a natural CTA into a relevant free analysis or demo.

Priority is **timely and citable**, not high publishing volume.

A useful public article can serve five functions simultaneously:

1. search discovery;
2. journalist/industry citation;
3. outbound follow-up material;
4. retargeting audience creation;
5. proof that TariffCompass understands the current issue.

## 10. Partnerships and distribution

Before 30+3, pursue partnerships only when they can produce customers without a long enterprise cycle.

Good early partnership targets:

- accounting firms;
- fractional CFO networks;
- chambers of commerce;
- manufacturing associations;
- import/export business groups;
- local/regional economic development organizations;
- business bankers and commercial lenders as informal referral sources;
- customs brokers/trade professionals as referral or credibility partners where interests align.

Avoid complex integrations, exclusivity deals, procurement-heavy government programs, and large institutional pilots before the initial customer target unless the opportunity is unusually easy and commercially meaningful.

## 11. Founding offer strategy

### Business

First 10 paying Business customers:

- C$49.50/month for first 12 months;
- C$99/month thereafter.

Use scarcity truthfully: only 10 seats.

Do not make the discount the headline value proposition.

### Advisor

First 3 paying Advisor customers:

- C$124.50/month for first 12 months;
- C$249/month thereafter.

The offer compensates for being an early design partner, not for weak product value.

Track founding seats explicitly so the offer cannot be accidentally over-allocated.

## 12. Customer interview and evidence program

Interview every founding customer and a sample of later customers.

Capture:

- problem trigger;
- cross-border trade profile;
- materiality of exposure;
- feature/value that drove purchase;
- pricing reaction;
- objection before purchase;
- expected ongoing use;
- cancellation risk;
- desired alert cadence;
- referral willingness;
- testimonial permission.

By the 30+3 milestone, aim to have:

- 10+ documented interview summaries;
- 5+ usable Business quotes;
- 2–3 strong case studies/customer stories;
- at least 1 Advisor story;
- evidence of at least one genuinely useful alert/change event if the tariff environment provides one.

These become both product-learning assets and future crowdfunding evidence.

## 14. Milestone roadmap

### Milestone 0 — Beta ready

Requirements:

- six C$99 launch gates in `PENDING.md` credible;
- C$99 Business billing live and tested;
- founding offer operational;
- legal/commercial minimum cleared;
- 30+ qualified prospects ready for launch outreach.

### Milestone 1 — 5 paying Business customers

Objective: verify someone outside the founding team will pay.

Actions:

- interview all 5;
- fix major conversion blockers;
- do not scale ads yet unless conversion is already strong.

### Milestone 2 — 10 paying Business customers

Objective: complete founding Business cohort.

Actions:

- document customer stories;
- identify best-performing segment;
- identify best acquisition source;
- begin testing small paid-search spend.

### Milestone 3 — 20 paying Business customers

Objective: validate C$99 standard pricing.

Actions:

- majority of new sales should now be at full price;
- expand the winning outbound segment;
- scale only paid channels with credible CAC/payback;
- begin stronger accountant/advisor design-partner motion.

### Milestone 4 — 30 paying Business customers

Objective: complete Business side of crowdfunding-readiness target.

Actions:

- preserve retention;
- capture strongest 2–3 customer stories;
- avoid unnecessary feature expansion;
- prioritize first 3 Advisor conversions.

### Milestone 5 — 30 Business + 3 Advisor

Commercial target:

- 33 paying accounts;
- C$3,717 normalized MRR / C$44,604 normalized ARR at standard pricing;
- actual billed revenue tracked separately;
- 2–3 credible customer stories;
- at least one strong Advisor proof point if possible.

At this milestone, move crowdfunding preparation from `FUTURE.md` into the active plan.

---

## 15. What not to do before 30+3

Do not dilute the acquisition effort with:

- broad consumer awareness campaigns;
- generic social-media posting schedules;
- expensive sponsorships;
- national brand campaigns;
- large PR retainers;
- enterprise sales teams;
- complex affiliate software;
- mass cold-email blasts with poor targeting;
- building features solely to impress investors;
- institutional sales cycles that consume founder time without near-term revenue;
- high-volume content production that does not drive trust, citations, demos or conversion.

---

## 16. Governing rule

Before approving a marketing activity, ask:

> **Will this materially increase the probability of acquiring the next paying Business or Advisor customer at acceptable economics?**

If not, defer it.

The marketing strategy ends when the initial target is reached:

> **30 Business + 3 Advisor paying accounts.**

At that point, re-underwrite channels, pricing, retention, product-market fit, fundraising/crowdfunding, and the next growth target using actual customer data rather than pre-launch assumptions.
