# TariffCompass — Demo Storyboard

_Last updated: 28 August 2026_

This is the canonical Phase 1 story-driven demo for TariffCompass. Its purpose is to make the C$99/month value proposition immediately understandable to a Canadian trade-exposed SME.

The first implementation should be a **scrollable web story / guided product walkthrough**. Once the story and product flow are stable, the same narrative can be reused for a short video, sales deck, founder demo, PDF leave-behind and future fundraising materials.

## Demo objective

Within roughly two minutes, a prospect should understand:

1. who TariffCompass is for;
2. what business problem it solves;
3. how a customer uses it;
4. what specific financial/trade intelligence the customer receives;
5. why the information can be trusted; and
6. why paying C$99/month is rational.

The demo should not try to explain every feature. It should tell one coherent story.

---

# Flagship persona — Bob

**Bob** is the owner of a small-to-medium Ontario auto-parts manufacturer.

Illustrative profile for storytelling only:

- Location: Ontario, Canada
- Business: manufacturer of automotive components
- Primary trade direction: exports from Canada
- Approximate annual exports: C$2.0 million
- U.S. customers: 70% / C$1.4 million
- European customers: 20% / C$400,000
- Japan customers: 10% / C$200,000
- Products: one or more auto-parts HS classifications relevant to the live demo

All demo tariff rates, effective dates, trade measures, source citations and calculated dollar exposures must come from real TariffCompass data at demo time. **Never invent a tariff rate merely to make the story more dramatic.** If the selected auto-parts example does not produce a compelling sourced result, use another real Canadian manufacturing product while preserving the same story structure.

# Scene 1 — Meet Bob

## Core message

> **Bob runs a Canadian auto-parts business. Most of his revenue depends on cross-border trade.**

## Story

Bob manufactures automotive components in Ontario. About 70% of his exports go to customers in the United States, 20% to Europe, and 10% to Japan.

A tariff or trade-policy change in any one of those markets can affect pricing, customer demand, margins and cash flow.

## Visual

Show a clean customer/business card with:

- Ontario
- Auto parts
- C$2.0M annual exports
- U.S. 70%
- Europe 20%
- Japan 10%

A simple exposure chart or market split may be used, but the story must remain legible without it.

## Prospect takeaway

> “This looks like a real Canadian SME, not a theoretical trade example.”

---

# Scene 2 — Bob's problem

## Core message

> **Bob does not need more tariff news. He needs to know what affects his business.**

## Story

A headline says a tariff has changed. Another government notice appears later. A customs notice explains implementation. Bob may also hear from his accountant, banker, broker or industry association.

But the business questions remain:

- Does this apply to my product?
- Which of my markets is affected?
- When does the change take effect?
- How much money could be at risk?
- Has the treatment changed from what it was before?
- What should I investigate next?

Bob should not have to check numerous government sites and news sources every morning just to answer those questions.

## Visual

Show several source types/news/government-notice cards converging toward a single unresolved question:

**“What does this mean for my business?”**

Do not depict TariffCompass as replacing a customs broker or legal professional.

---

# Scene 3 — Bob sets up TariffCompass

## Core message

> **Bob tells TariffCompass what he trades and where.**

## Live product flow

Show Bob entering or confirming:

- product description;
- HS code or likely HS heading/code;
- export/import direction;
- destination/origin market;
- approximate annual trade value; and
- saved exposure / monitoring preference.

For the flagship story, Bob should save at least the U.S. exposure and, where supported by reliable data, Europe and Japan.

## Product principle

The user should not need to create a complete ERP-style trade record. Intake should be light enough that an owner can get to value quickly.

## Prospect takeaway

> “I could set this up for my own products.”

---

# Scene 4 — Bob sees his trade-exposure dashboard

## Core message

> **TariffCompass turns Bob's trade profile into a business-specific view of tariff risk.**

This is the most important scene in the demo.

## Dashboard summary

Show a personalized dashboard built around **saved exposures**, not a generic company homepage and not a separate dashboard for every HS code.

Recommended top-level cards:

- Total monitored trade value
- Products / HS codes monitored
- Estimated tariff exposure
- Material alerts since last login
- Upcoming effective/review/deadline dates

## Saved-exposure view

For each material product/route, show as much of the following as reliable data allows:

| Field | Example purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Product / HS code | What Bob trades |
| Trade route | Canada → U.S., Canada → EU, etc. |
| Current treatment | Current applicable tariff/trade measure |
| Previous treatment | Before/after context where a change occurred |
| Effective date | When the current treatment applies |
| Annual trade value | Bob's own exposure input |
| Estimated dollar impact | Planning estimate of financial exposure |
| Confidence | Official / estimated / review needed or final adopted model |
| Source | Direct provenance link |

## Dynamic information

The dashboard should surface:

- new relevant trade-policy changes;
- upcoming effective dates;
- expiring/review dates where material;
- relevant government-program deadlines where verified;
- changes in saved-product exposure; and
- practical next actions.

## Prospect takeaway

> “This is not generic tariff information. It is my products, my markets and my money.”

# Scene 5 — What changed?

## Core message

> **When a measure changes, Bob sees the change in business terms.**

Use one real, sourced example in the live demo.

Show:

- what changed;
- prior treatment;
- current treatment;
- effective date;
- affected saved product / HS code;
- estimated dollar impact based on Bob's trade value;
- source/legal or administrative authority; and
- confidence/status.

If no material recent change exists for the selected auto-parts code, choose another live product example rather than fabricating a change.

## Prospect takeaway

> “I would want to know this before I discover it through a customer, supplier or broker.”

---

# Scene 6 — Bob can verify the source

## Core message

> **Bob can see where TariffCompass got the information and how it became his result.**

Link directly into the public **Sources & Methodology** page.

Show the provenance flow:

**Official source detected → measure identified → affected HS codes/products mapped → origin/destination/applicability checked → effective date recorded → confidence assigned → Bob's saved exposure matched → result/alert displayed**

Emphasize:

- legal/operative sources;
- administrative/implementation sources;
- official announcement/context sources;
- monitoring frequency / last checked; and
- media used only as supplemental discovery/context.

## Prospect takeaway

> “This is a sourced trade-intelligence system, not a generic AI answer.”

---

# Scene 7 — What can Bob do next?

## Core message

> **TariffCompass helps Bob decide what to investigate next.**

Depending on the sourced data, show practical response intelligence such as:

- compare alternative export markets;
- examine alternative sourcing routes where relevant;
- review CUSMA / CETA / CPTPP implications;
- review verified government support programs/deadlines;
- model simple pricing/margin implications; and
- prepare questions for a customs broker, accountant, lawyer or management meeting.

Do not imply TariffCompass replaces professional customs/legal/tax advice.

## Prospect takeaway

> “The platform does not stop at telling me there is a problem.”

---

# Scene 8 — Why Bob pays C$99/month

## Core message

> **For Bob, C$99/month is the cost of staying on top of the trade exposures that matter to his business.**

Summarize the recurring value:

- monitor saved products / HS codes;
- understand what changed;
- see source and effective date;
- quantify approximate dollar impact;
- track important dates;
- compare credible responses; and
- receive alerts rather than manually searching for changes.

Suggested closing copy:

> **Instead of chasing tariff updates across multiple sources, Bob logs in and sees what matters to his business. TariffCompass keeps watching when he is not logged in.**

CTA:

**Monitor your trade exposure — C$99/month**

Founding-offer copy may be shown only while the approved first-10 Business offer remains available.

# Demo-dashboard product requirements implied by the story

The story is not merely marketing; it should pressure-test whether the actual paid product is worth C$99.

The Business dashboard should ultimately be organized around a customer's **saved trade exposures**.

Minimum high-value dashboard components:

1. **Exposure summary** — total monitored trade value and estimated exposure.
2. **Saved products / routes** — product + HS code + origin/destination + annual value.
3. **Recent changes** — material before/after tariff or trade-policy changes affecting those exposures.
4. **Upcoming dates** — effective, expiry, review and verified program dates relevant to the customer.
5. **Alerts / action queue** — what needs attention now.
6. **Source confidence** — direct provenance visible on material results.
7. **Response intelligence** — credible next actions and alternatives based on available data.

Avoid turning the dashboard into a broad analytics suite before C$2,500 MRR.

# Delivery format sequence

## 1. Beta/V1 — scrollable web story

Build first because it is:

- inexpensive;
- easy to update as the product changes;
- usable in live sales calls;
- shareable from the website;
- convertible into screenshots and outreach collateral; and
- reusable later for video/fundraising.

The first web version can be largely guided/static storytelling with selected live product surfaces embedded or reproduced accurately. Do not build a complex demo framework if a simple page tells the story.

## 2. After story validation — founder sales deck

Reuse the same eight scenes for a short sales presentation.

## 3. After product flow stabilizes — 60–120 second video

Record/narrate the proven story. Do not spend heavily on video before the actual C$99 workflow and messaging are stable.

## 4. Later reuse

The same narrative can support:

- customer onboarding;
- accountant/advisor demonstrations;
- public presentations;
- media explanations; and
- future crowdfunding or investor materials if that path is pursued.

# Demo integrity rules

- The persona and trade-value split may be illustrative and must be labelled accordingly where necessary.
- Tariff rates, effective dates, HS applicability, legal instruments and program deadlines shown as facts must be real and sourced.
- Never create a dramatic fictional tariff event just to improve the demo.
- If auto parts is not the strongest live example, change the product example rather than weakening data integrity.
- The demo must clearly distinguish planning exposure estimates from final customs-duty determinations.
- The demo should reinforce, not contradict, `BUSINESS_PLAN.md`, `ROADMAP.md`, `PENDING.md` and the Sources & Methodology trust model.

# Success test

After the walkthrough, ask a prospect:

> **“If TariffCompass did this reliably for your products and markets, would C$99/month feel worth it?”**

The demo succeeds when prospects can understand the value without a long explanation and naturally translate Bob's story into their own business.
