# TariffCompass — Capital Raising Strategy

_Last updated: 27 August 2026_

This document is the canonical home for TariffCompass financing strategy, crowdfunding preparation, valuation/dilution assumptions, use of proceeds, founder compensation policy after financing, and capital-allocation discipline.

Canonical commercial/product strategy remains [`BUSINESS_PLAN.md`](BUSINESS_PLAN.md). The acquisition roadmap remains [`MARKETING_STRATEGY.md`](MARKETING_STRATEGY.md). Beta/V1 launch blockers remain in [`PENDING.md`](PENDING.md).

# 1. Capital strategy objective

TariffCompass should raise only enough outside capital to accelerate a business that has already shown genuine customer willingness to pay.

The preferred sequence is:

**credible Business Beta/V1 → paying customers → C$2,500 MRR / C$30,000 ARR → build toward 30 Business + 3 Advisor customers + 2–3 strong customer stories → launch crowdfunding / angel round.**

The financing should fund market scale, not product discovery from zero.

The company does not need to become venture-scale to be successful. A strong outcome can be a lean, profitable, founder-controlled Canadian SaaS company with recurring revenue, disciplined reinvestment, defensible founder compensation and eventual dividends.

# 2. Current planned raise

Current planning assumption:

- **Target raise:** approximately **C$300,000**
- **Indicative valuation:** approximately **C$3.0 million**
- **Preferred channel:** equity crowdfunding, potentially combined with angels
- **Timing:** after meaningful paying-customer proof, not before the Business Beta/V1 is commercially credible

Valuation should be reassessed immediately before launch based on:

- actual MRR/ARR;
- customer count;
- retention/churn;
- growth rate;
- Advisor traction;
- customer concentration;
- product defensibility;
- public authority/citation traction;
- current tariff-policy environment; and
- prevailing private-market conditions.

The C$3 million valuation is a planning assumption, not a commitment.

## Indicative dilution

If C$300,000 is raised at a C$3.0 million **pre-money** valuation, indicative post-money ownership sold would be approximately **9.1%**.

If C$3.0 million is quoted as a **post-money** valuation, dilution would be **10%**.

Final structure, security type and investor rights require securities/legal advice.

# 3. Crowdfunding readiness gate

The preferred crowdfunding-readiness milestone is:

- **30 paying TariffCompass Business customers**;
- **3 paying TariffCompass Advisor customers**;
- approximately **2–3 strong customer stories/testimonials**;
- a credible live product;
- repeat usage / evidence of monitoring value; and
- clear revenue reporting that distinguishes actual billed MRR from normalized/full-price MRR.

At standard pricing:

- 30 Business × C$99 = **C$2,970 normalized MRR**;
- 3 Advisor × C$249 = **C$747 normalized MRR**;
- total = **C$3,717 normalized MRR / C$44,604 normalized ARR**.

Actual billed MRR will be lower while founding discounts apply and must never be represented as full-price recurring revenue.

Preparation may begin before the full gate is reached if momentum is clearly visible, but the campaign should not distract from selling the product.

# 4. Crowdfunding investment thesis

The campaign should remain simple and legible to a retail investor.

Core narrative:

> **TariffCompass turns tariffs and trade-policy changes into actionable intelligence for Canadian businesses and the professionals who advise them.**

The one-screen proof should show:

1. a real Canadian company/product exposure;
2. a real tariff/trade-policy change;
3. the official source and effective date;
4. the estimated financial impact;
5. clear response options; and
6. ongoing monitoring/alerting.

The campaign should emphasize that TariffCompass is not merely an AI wrapper. The durable asset is the structured, sourced, historically versioned trade-intelligence layer mapped to company exposure.

Public authority and media citations may strengthen the campaign, but paying-customer proof remains more important than traffic or press coverage.

# 6. Use of proceeds — base case

The C$300,000 raise should primarily buy **time and distribution**, not organizational complexity.

Indicative use-of-proceeds philosophy:

- founder/CEO salary at a deliberately below-market level initially;
- product/data reliability and official-source ingestion;
- contractors / fractional technical specialists where necessary;
- measurable customer acquisition;
- legal, accounting, insurance and financing costs;
- infrastructure/software/data costs; and
- a meaningful liquidity reserve.

Do not use the initial round to build a large permanent team before repeatable sales and retention are visible.

Do not create investor-facing product features merely because the capital is raised through crowdfunding.

# 8. Founder compensation policy after financing

Founder compensation should be **milestone-based, not calendar-based**.

The founder should accept an early entrepreneurial discount, but the company should not rely on permanent founder underpayment as an operating model.

A useful external personal market anchor is the founder's last employed compensation of approximately **C$135,000 annually** before leaving Mercer in October 2025. The objective is not to restore that salary immediately; it is to establish a defensible path back to market compensation as the company becomes economically capable of supporting it.

## Proposed founder/CEO salary ladder

| Company milestone | Founder/CEO base salary |
|---|---:|
| Successful financing / early PMF | **C$85,000** |
| C$150K ARR + at least 15 months projected runway | **C$100,000** |
| C$250K ARR + acceptable retention + at least 12 months runway | **C$120,000** |
| C$500K ARR or sustained positive EBITDA, with at least 12 months liquidity | **C$135,000** |
| C$750K–C$1M ARR + positive EBITDA | **C$150,000–C$165,000** |
| C$1.5M–C$2M+ ARR + sustained profitability | **C$175,000–C$200,000**, subject to board/investor governance |

The salary trigger should require both the revenue milestone and adequate liquidity/runway where specified.

No automatic annual raises merely because a year has passed.

## Compensation governance

Until sustainable positive cash flow is achieved:

- no founder bonus;
- no founder dividend;
- founder salary increases should be documented against the agreed milestone policy; and
- founder secondary liquidity should follow the separate adopted secondary policy below rather than being treated as compensation.

Once the business is sustainably profitable, compensation should be reviewed against the role actually being performed, company scale, market data, retained ownership and cash needs.

# 9. Market-scale trigger

The company should begin thinking in terms of **market-scale operations**, rather than founder-led survival, when the following are broadly true:

- approximately **C$500K ARR or higher**;
- credible retention / low churn;
- repeatable customer acquisition;
- gross margin remains strong;
- at least 12 months forward liquidity after planned hiring; and
- positive or near-positive EBITDA.

At this stage it becomes defensible to:

- restore founder salary toward/above the prior C$135K professional-market benchmark;
- make selective permanent hires instead of relying mostly on contractors;
- scale paid acquisition channels with measured CAC/payback;
- invest more materially in the Advisor channel and institutional opportunities; and
- reconsider whether another financing round would accelerate value creation economically.

Do not equate "market scale" with indiscriminate hiring.

---

# 10. Capital allocation discipline

Until the business is sustainably profitable:

1. Protect liquidity and runway.
2. Fund product/data reliability before cosmetic expansion.
3. Fund customer acquisition only when channel economics are measurable.
4. Prefer variable/fractional specialist cost before adding large fixed payroll.
5. Do not pursue broad institutional features without real customer pull.
6. Do not raise a second round merely because one is available.
7. Do not present normalized MRR as billed revenue.
8. Maintain a clear distinction between operating cash, financing proceeds and distributable profit.

# 11. Dividends and founder liquidity

The long-term goal may include founder dividends because TariffCompass is intended to be capable of becoming a profitable owner-operated SaaS business.

However, dividends should begin only when:

- the company has sustainable positive free cash flow;
- tax obligations and working-capital needs are covered;
- at least 12 months of forward operating liquidity remains after the proposed distribution; and
- the distribution does not materially impair an attractive growth opportunity.

## Adopted founder-secondary policy

Founder secondary sales are separate from salary and dividends. They should be used sparingly and structured so they do not weaken investor confidence or deprive the company of needed growth capital.

### First crowdfunding round

Preferred position:

- **No planned founder secondary** in the first C$300K crowdfunding/angel financing.
- Market the round as **100% primary capital** for the company.

Earliest defensible exception:

- If investor demand materially exceeds the company's actual capital requirement and the round is clearly strong/oversubscribed, allow up to approximately **C$25K–C$40K** of founder secondary liquidity.
- This should be explicitly approved and transparently disclosed.
- It should not cause the company to receive less than the primary capital it actually needs.

### First planned secondary eligibility

A meaningful planned founder secondary becomes eligible when TariffCompass reaches approximately:

- **C$250K ARR**;
- good/credible retention;
- and at least **18 months projected post-transaction runway**.

At that point, approximately **C$50K–C$100K** of founder liquidity can be considered, subject to investor demand, ownership/dilution, tax and governance.

### Meaningful founder-liquidity stage

At approximately:

- **C$500K ARR**;
- positive EBITDA or clearly sustainable economics; and
- adequate post-transaction liquidity,

approximately **C$100K–C$200K** of founder secondary may be reasonable if supported by a financing transaction and investor demand.

At **C$1M+ ARR** with profitability or a larger institutional financing, a more substantial structured secondary can be evaluated based on cap-table economics, retained founder ownership and the company's capital needs.

### Secondary decision rules

Before any founder secondary, confirm:

1. the company receives the primary capital it genuinely needs;
2. post-transaction runway remains appropriate;
3. the founder retains strong long-term economic alignment and control;
4. the secondary amount is reasonable relative to the size/stage of the financing;
5. investor demand genuinely supports the transaction;
6. required securities, shareholder and board approvals are obtained; and
7. legal and tax consequences are reviewed.

The objective is **measured founder de-risking after value creation**, not early cash-out.

---

# 12. Future financing decision rule

A second financing round is not automatically part of the plan.

Raise again only if the expected return from accelerating growth is clearly superior to remaining self-funded from recurring revenue.

Before any future raise, ask:

> **What specific growth constraint does new capital remove, what measurable outcome should the capital produce, and is the expected value creation greater than the dilution?**

If that answer is weak, do not raise.

# 14. Standing principles

- Financing serves the business; the business does not exist to raise financing.
- Customer traction should precede meaningful dilution.
- Keep the first raise modest and capital-efficient.
- Founder compensation should be below market early, but should normalize as the company earns the ability to pay it.
- Founder liquidity should follow value creation and preserve strong alignment with investors.
- Protect founder control unless dilution has a compelling economic justification.
- Crowdfunding should function as both financing and distribution, but never substitute for product-market fit.
- Build a company capable of surviving without another round.
