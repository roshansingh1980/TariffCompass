# TariffCompass — Business Plan

_Last updated: 27 August 2026_

## 1. Executive summary

TariffCompass is a Canadian trade-impact intelligence platform for businesses that import, export, or do both, and for the accountants and fractional-CFO/advisory firms that serve them.

The product answers four practical questions:

1. **What changed?**
2. **Does it affect me or my clients?**
3. **What is the estimated financial impact?**
4. **What are the most relevant response options?**

TariffCompass is not intended to become a full customs-compliance workflow suite in Phase 1. The product should remain narrowly focused on converting current, sourced tariff and trade-policy information into company-specific decision intelligence.

The immediate commercial objective is **C$2,500 MRR / C$30,000 ARR as quickly as possible**. Every Phase 1 product, pricing, sales, and content decision should be evaluated against whether it materially improves conversion, retention, willingness to pay, or the data reliability required for the paid product.

## 2. Phase 1 customer strategy

### Paid segment A — Canadian trade-exposed SMEs

One commercial segment covering:

- importers;
- exporters; and
- businesses that both import and export.

Positioning: **For Canadian businesses that buy or sell across borders.**

The product should not create separate importer and exporter businesses. Trade direction is an input to the analysis, not a separate company strategy.

### Paid segment B — accountants and fractional CFO/advisory firms

Accountants and fractional CFO/advisory firms are the first professional segment because they:

- already have trusted recurring relationships with SMEs;
- see tariff impacts in margins, inventory costs, forecasts, financing requirements, and cash flow;
- can monitor multiple affected clients from one account; and
- can become both paying customers and distribution partners.

General consultants, lawyers, customs professionals, banks, associations, and institutional users are not active Phase 1 target segments unless an opportunistic sale is unusually easy.

### Free public audience

Journalists, lawyers, policymakers, politicians, researchers, associations, consultants, businesses, and the general public may use the public information layer for free.

The goal is to make TariffCompass useful and citable through:

- short, data-driven insights;
- dated tariff and trade-policy updates;
- transparent source attribution;
- methodology notes; and
- reusable public tables/charts where inexpensive to maintain.

This is a credibility and distribution layer, not a separate Phase 1 revenue business.

**Operating principle:** public information creates authority; private relevance creates revenue.

## 3. Product proposition

### Core promise

**TariffCompass tells a Canadian business what trade-policy changes affect it, estimates the financial impact, and identifies the most relevant response options.**

### Core workflow

A user provides, where available:

- product description;
- HS code;
- import/export direction;
- origin and destination;
- annual trade value;
- province; and
- optional business context.

TariffCompass returns:

- current applicable tariff/trade measures and confidence;
- source and effective date;
- before/after change where known;
- estimated dollar exposure;
- market or sourcing comparisons;
- relevant trade-agreement implications;
- government programs or deadlines worth reviewing;
- clear next actions; and
- monitoring for future changes on paid plans.

### The Phase 1 “wow”

Specificity, financial relevance, and monitoring.

A strong demonstration should be understandable in under a minute:

> This Canadian company imports X and exports Y. A tariff changed. TariffCompass shows which product is affected, the sourced legal change, the estimated annual financial impact, and the response options worth investigating.

## 4. Phase 1 product scope

Build only the capabilities needed to reach the first C$2,500 MRR.

### Must have

1. **Import + export exposure**
   - HS/product-level analysis where reliable data exists.
   - Canada remains the home country.

2. **Financial impact**
   - Convert rates and trade value into estimated dollar exposure.
   - Make financial impact prominent, not buried in a report.

3. **Change monitoring**
   - Effective-date history and rate/policy changes.
   - Saved-product monitoring and meaningful alerts.

4. **Response intelligence**
   - Alternative sourcing/destination comparisons.
   - Relevant FTA implications and government-support programs.
   - Clear boundary between decision support and professional customs/legal advice.

5. **Advisor portfolio view**
   - Multiple client profiles.
   - Client Exposure Radar showing which clients were affected by recent changes and estimated exposure.
   - Client-ready reports and, when justified, white-label output.

6. **Public authority layer — lightweight**
   - Insights, Updates, Sources, citable methodology and dated data.
   - Cap this at approximately 10–15% of Phase 1 effort.

### Explicitly not Phase 1

- full customs-entry workflow;
- supplier certificate/document repository;
- immutable CBSA audit platform;
- comprehensive origin-document management;
- journalist or politician dashboards;
- dedicated lawyer workflows;
- bank-wide portfolio analytics;
- institutional APIs;
- broad international home-country support; and
- features that do not materially improve the probability of C$2,500 MRR.

## 5. Pricing

Pricing is designed around fast ARR acquisition, simple buying decisions, and low customer-count requirements.

### Free

Public tariff intelligence, selected market/risk information, insights, updates, sources, and a useful limited analysis experience.

Purpose: discovery, trust, SEO, citations, referrals, and conversion.

### TariffCompass Business — C$99/month

Target customer: Canadian importer/exporter SME.

Expected Phase 1 value:

- saved trade profiles/products;
- estimated dollar exposure;
- tariff/trade-policy monitoring;
- alerts;
- response options;
- deeper program information; and
- shareable analysis/brief.

### TariffCompass Advisor — C$249/month

Target customer: accountant or fractional CFO/advisory firm.

Expected Phase 1 value:

- multi-client workspace;
- Client Exposure Radar;
- alerts across client portfolios;
- client-ready reports;
- higher profile limits; and
- white-label capability when sufficiently robust.

Do not add more subscription tiers during Phase 1.

### Founding-customer offers

**Founding Customer Offer:** the first 10 paying TariffCompass Business customers receive 50% off their first 12 months: approximately **C$49.50/month for 12 months**, then the standard **C$99/month** applies automatically. This is not a lifetime discount.

**Founding Advisor Offer:** the first 3 paying TariffCompass Advisor customers receive 50% off their first 12 months: approximately **C$124.50/month for 12 months**, then the standard **C$249/month** applies automatically. This is not a lifetime discount.

These offers must not be represented as active in the product until the corresponding Stripe billing configuration exists. Stripe remains the billing authority.

## 6. ARR target and customer math

The immediate milestone is:

- **C$2,500 MRR**
- **C$30,000 ARR run-rate**

The next commercial/crowdfunding-readiness milestone is 30 paying Business customers, 3 paying Advisor accounts, and approximately 2–3 strong customer stories or testimonials.

At full standard pricing, that mix is:

- 30 Business accounts × C$99 = C$2,970 MRR;
- 3 Advisor accounts × C$249 = C$747 MRR; and
- **C$3,717 normalized MRR / C$44,604 normalized ARR**.

Actual billed MRR and contracted ARR will be lower while founding discounts apply. Reporting must distinguish actual billed recurring revenue from standard-price or normalized MRR and must never present discounted subscriptions as full-price revenue.

## 7. Early reinvestment policy

Until the initial C$2,500 MRR milestone is reached:

- pay no founder dividends;
- keep operating costs lean;
- recycle available operating cash and gross profit into customer acquisition and product/data reliability;
- use advertising and acquisition channels only when results are measurable; and
- track CAC and conversion rather than spending blindly.

## 8. Go-to-market

### Business sales motion

Lead with a concrete exposure question:

> Give us one product you import or export and your approximate annual trade value. TariffCompass will show the current exposure, the source behind it, and what changed.

If material exposure exists, the paid conversion proposition is monitoring:

> TariffCompass will keep watching this product and tell you when the underlying tariff treatment changes and what that could mean financially.

### Advisor sales motion

Lead with portfolio relevance:

> TariffCompass shows which of your clients were affected by trade-policy changes and the estimated financial exposure, so you know who needs a conversation first.

### Initial acquisition

Prioritize direct founder-led sales and demos over broad paid marketing until conversion and retention are understood.

Target conversations:

- Canadian manufacturers;
- wholesalers/distributors;
- import-dependent retailers;
- exporters with material U.S. exposure;
- accounting firms with SME commercial clients; and
- fractional CFO firms.

## 9. Public intelligence and brand strategy

TariffCompass should become a credible, citable Canadian source of tariff and trade-impact information without becoming a media company.

Public content should be:

- short;
- quantitative;
- sourced;
- dated;
- explicit about methodology and uncertainty; and
- reusable by journalists, lawyers, researchers, and policymakers.

Examples:

- Which Canadian sectors are currently most exposed to a new U.S. tariff action?
- What changed in Section 232 treatment this month?
- Which Canadian import categories are most affected by current counter-tariffs?
- What a specific tariff change means for a representative Canadian SME.

The public history of a tariff change can remain free. The paid value is identifying that the change affects **your products or your clients** and quantifying that impact.

## 10. Competitive positioning

TariffTrail validates willingness to pay for Canadian tariff software and has a strong importer/compliance orientation.

TariffCompass should not attempt to win by cloning a full import-compliance suite. Its differentiated centre is the **Canadian business**, not only the customs entry:

- imports and exports in one exposure view;
- company-specific financial impact;
- trade-policy change monitoring;
- response scenarios; and
- advisor portfolio intelligence.

The competitive test is not “do we have every compliance feature?” It is “can TariffCompass tell a business or advisor something commercially important faster and more clearly?”

## 11. Data and technology strategy

The durable asset is the structured trade-intelligence layer, not the LLM-generated prose.

Longer-term architecture should model:

- trade measures and legal instruments;
- applicability by HS code, origin, destination, exclusions and thresholds;
- base/preferential/additional rates and stacking;
- effective-date history; and
- company/client trade exposure.

Conceptually:

**Trade Measure × Company Exposure = Impact**

AI may summarize and explain that structured intelligence, but it must not invent tariff rates, effective dates, program eligibility, or legal conclusions.

## 12. Success metrics

Primary:

- MRR;
- ARR;
- paying Business accounts;
- paying Advisor accounts;
- time from first demo to payment;
- demo/trial-to-paid conversion;
- ARPA; and
- retention/churn.

Product-value metrics:

- number of monitored products/profiles;
- number of material alerts delivered;
- number of clients affected per Advisor account; and
- repeated use after a tariff change.

Public-authority metrics are secondary until the initial C$2,500 MRR milestone:

- citations/backlinks;
- organic traffic;
- newsletter/media references; and
- source-page usage.

## 13. Crowdfunding optionality

TariffCompass is a strong potential crowdfunding candidate because the problem and product can be understood visually and quickly by retail investors.

Crowdfunding is **not** a Phase 1 objective. The preferred sequence is:

1. build the focused product;
2. obtain paying Business and Advisor customers;
3. reach C$2,500 MRR;
4. build toward 30 Business customers, 3 Advisor customers, and 2–3 credible customer stories; and
5. then prepare and evaluate an equity crowdfunding launch, potentially alongside angels.

A future campaign can use the same demo that sells the product: a Canadian business, a real tariff change, a sourced financial impact, and a clear response.

The current planning assumption is a potential raise of approximately **C$300,000 at approximately a C$3 million valuation**, subject to later review. Crowdfunding remains strategic financing/distribution optionality, not a reason to broaden the first build or add investor functionality.

## 14. Phase 1 decision rule

Before approving a feature, campaign, integration, or customer segment, ask:

> **Does this materially improve our probability of reaching C$2,500 MRR quickly, while preserving the data foundation we will need if the product succeeds?**

If not, defer it.
